**Reading.** Within the bins the estimator predicts, the guarantee holds as the theory says (YOLOv8x @640, group, clean, argmax bins: held-out miss per predicted bin α = 0.1: 0.082–0.092; α = 0.2: 0.182–0.198). But it does not carry over to the true weather condition: on images that really are foggy the predicted-bin policy misses 0.112 at α = 0.1 (exceeds α in 70% of splits), 0.234 at α = 0.2 (exceeds α in 80% of splits), against 0.085, 0.193 for the oracle fog bin. The classifier puts 149 of 256 fog test-and-calibration images (mean per split) in its fog bin, alongside 100 sand images; the fog bin's threshold is set for that mixture and is too high for real fog. *A wrong bin costs false positives, not safety* holds for the predicted bin's average, not for the hazardous condition inside it: protecting fog needs an estimator that isolates fog (more fog training data, e.g. WEDGE or Foggy Cityscapes, or a visibility regressor) or a conservative rule that sends uncertain images to the strictest bin.
